Killing Bridges with Complexity, and Other Ways MMO Devs Spend Your Time

Null-sec’s jump highways are about to be gated by spreadsheets. While EVE’s designers tinker with capacitors and zones, elsewhere players are solving travel and time the old-fashioned way: swapping to bards for ports, mining 9,000 slate by hand, building mobile carriers, and quietly out-grinding everyone in “casual” WoW content.

CCP won’t delete EVE’s Ansiblex jump bridges; they’re going to make them unusable in interesting new ways. Wilhelm walked through the capacitor-and-zones plan — free jumps only if the destination is within 5 LY of your alliance capital, escalating multipliers all the way to 15x beyond 20 LY — paired with alliance-only access and no capitals. He’s less mad than practical: bridges that randomly lack charge strand pilots, route planners won’t keep up, and anything past Zone 2 dies to casual traffic. If you want to kill a system, make it unreliable. Wilhelm on The Ancient Gaming Noob

The coalition knock-on is obvious. Ansiblex access stops at your alliance border, which means coalition fleets can’t stage through partner space on bridges anymore. Wilhelm’s read is that this doesn’t liberate small alliances; it isolates them. Malcanis’ Law hasn’t been repealed, and the rich get the workarounds. Brave didn’t join the Imperium for the vibes; they joined because getting farmed alone in the north sucked. Alliance-only bridges and no caps make the “long way around” the new norm for anyone not anchored on a giant. Wilhelm on The Ancient Gaming Noob

What really rankles in his follow-up is the dev-time burn. If the goal is to gut force projection, just turn off the structures and refund the parts, he says. Instead we’re getting capacitor mini-games, UI that probably won’t surface charge states well, and an experience tuned for nano-gang tourists while home-defense logistics become a dice roll. Reliable actions yielding reliable outcomes is the thing players bank on; turn that into RNG and they’ll just stop using the tool. Wilhelm on The Ancient Gaming Noob

That “who respects my time?” theme ran through other posts this week. In SWTOR, Shintar loved the scope of Legacy Reborn — big cast, multiple POV swaps — but called out a new on-rails space interlude that asks you to tab-target off-screen objects while the camera snaps back to forward view. The ground Basilisk droid section fared better, and the update swings big enough to kill Shae and let you crawl through Malgus’ head… only to strand you in a hard cut to Odessen after an orbital bombardment cliffhanger. Great drama, awkward MMO handoff. Shintar on Going Commando

Over in EverQuest Legends, Tipa and Kasul did the civilized thing and solved travel with class picks. Ports now, ports later: he’ll open with wizard then swap to bard; she starts druid then flips to enchanter after they’ve unlocked the world. In the meantime she’s a dwarf cleric with Shadow Knight secondary — good-side, strong enough to carry plate — and she’s already scouting faction work so an SK guildmaster will talk to her past 20. Figure out movement first; everything else is gravy. Tipa on Chasing Dings!

Daybreak, for its part, is keeping the spigots open. EverQuest Legends leaves beta and launches July 28; if you prefer your nostalgia mounted on rails, a new EQ TLP called Lethar opens August 26 starting at The Serpent’s Spine. Wilhelm’s “maybe later” reads honest — the beta sugar high is gone, balance passes landed, and sometimes summer isn’t the season for a 20-year time machine. Wilhelm on The Ancient Gaming Noob

Meanwhile, Stargrace is putting in the kind of hours game directors swear they can automate away. She just pushed mining past 90 in Wurm Online — roughly 9,000 slate shards’ worth — and is turning the pile into bricks for a castle on Deliverance while eyeing a 10-story build and a rift tomorrow. No capacitor, no zones; just a vein, a timer, and progress you can stack. Stargrace on A Pixel Nomad

And about all that “casual” content the industry says it’s building: Shintar’s Data for Azeroth check-in shows that far fewer players finish Decor Duel roles, Abyss Anglers grinds, or delves-as-healer than complete a season’s worth of M+10s or even the Voidspire capstone. She’s in the top few percent for mini-games while one in five sports the Myth of the Dawn achievement. The audience says what it values every patch; it’s not the picnic tables. Shintar on Priest with a Cause

Stargrace’s gold wrap-up read like the counterexample: double the server footprint to 20, cap posting to 90 minutes a day, and let recipes (and even an Expert Fishing book) carry the load. There’s grind there too, but it’s player-paced and profit shows up on the right side of the ledger the same day. Stargrace on A Pixel Nomad

If you want to see what “respect my time” looks like tuned to one person, look at Scopique’s Star Citizen fleet. He built a pocket industrial task force: an Ironclad carrier with a Salvation salvager and a Golem miner on the deck, stocked to refine and refuel in the field. When he’s not running the carrier, he’s mining in a Prospector with bigger hoppers, or hauling in a Hull B knowing the cargo’s billboarded on the hull. The MSR looks gorgeous and does nothing, but the ATLS GEO still eats rocks, and he can stay out until something explodes. Scopique on Scopique

Then there’s Neverness to Everness, where Bhagpuss found the city’s new “Midnight Catzebo” stands — giant cats sell scratch cards in an event called Mews Flash. Five price points, modest wins most of the time, the occasional 160k pop, and a pile of gift items even when you lose. Reddit’s already spinning plots about bankrupting players into buying Fons; he just liked the tactility of mousing off the foil in a thunderstorm and called it a day. Bhagpuss on Inventory Full

Back in EVE, the bridges aren’t dead yet, just conditional. Maybe CCP threads the UI needle and the math lands somewhere useful. More likely, people uncheck “use Ansiblex,” light a filament, and go do something that doesn’t ask them to bring a voltmeter to the bus stop. In other words: they’ll adapt — by avoiding the system you wanted them to use — and keep mining their 9,000 slate in peace. Wilhelm on The Ancient Gaming Noob
